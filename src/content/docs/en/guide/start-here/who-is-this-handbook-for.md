This handbook is mainly for people who are just beginning to use Codex seriously.

It does not assume you already know:

- The terminal
- Git
- Agents
- Prompt engineering
- Cloud environments

If you do, you may read faster. If not, the text aims to keep you up to speed.

## Four kinds of readers

| Reader | How you might use the handbook |
|---|---|
| AI beginner | Go from “what is this?” to a first safe task; terms are explained |
| Creator | Research, writing, presentations, and prototypes; start with the overview, then explore capability topics |
| Developer | Understand code, implement, test, review, and automate |
| Team | Shared instructions, permissions, quality, and governance (deeper coverage in later milestones) |

## These categories need not be rigid

Many people belong to two or three at once:

- You know some code but are new to using agents seriously
- You began as a content creator and now ask Codex to update a site
- You lead a team but first try Codex as an individual

These are four **use cases**, not mutually exclusive identities.

## Primary audience

The text is primarily written for readers **unfamiliar with terminals and agents**. Developer and team topics are not made superficial; they unfold progressively along learning paths.

## Common misconceptions

### 1. This handbook is not just for programmers

It may help if your work includes:

- Writing and organizing content
- Research and comparing information
- Maintaining documentation
- Prototyping and editing pages
- Clarifying team processes

### 2. Even if you know the basics, the opening pages are useful

“Before starting,” “Foundations,” and “First task” are not only for complete beginners. They also help you quickly calibrate:

- How Codex actually works
- How to assign a task
- Which risks to avoid upfront

### 3. You do not need to read from cover to cover

Many readers:

1. Read the introductory pages
2. Complete a first small task
3. Jump to pages relevant to their role

## What this handbook should not be treated as

- A complete official changelog for one product
- A collection of scripts to run in production without checking
- A replacement for your organization's security and compliance policies

## Who will find it useful

Read on if:

- You want to start using Codex safely
- You have some experience but your tasks still feel inconsistent
- You want to turn personal practices into a reusable team workflow
- You are not a developer but want to bring AI into everyday work
