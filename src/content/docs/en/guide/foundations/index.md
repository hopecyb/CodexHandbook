## Common misconceptions

### 1. Foundations are just theory, so I can skip them

You may get by briefly, but many later misunderstandings come from not knowing these concepts.

For example:

- Why Codex asks for approval
- Why context influences its behavior
- Why it might change an unexpected place
- Why “it says it is done” does not prove completion

### 2. If I know how to ask questions, I do not need these concepts

Good questions matter. Without knowing how Codex reads context, calls tools, and is constrained by permissions, however, it is harder to tell why a result drifted.

### 3. I should memorize definitions in this chapter

A more practical approach is to:

- First understand what problem each concept helps solve
- Then connect it to real situations in your work
